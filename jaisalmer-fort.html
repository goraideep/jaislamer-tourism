About the site

Jaisalmer Fort rises from Trikuta Hill above the Thar Desert. Its walls of yellow sandstone turn honey-gold in the late afternoon, which is why it is called the Sonar Quila, the Golden Fort. A ring of 99 bastions guards the outer wall, and visitors climb to the top through four massive gates: Akhai Pol, Suraj Pol, Ganesh Pol and Hawa Pol.

This is one of the few "living forts" in the world. Thousands of people still live inside its walls, in narrow lanes lined with houses, shops, guesthouses, Jain temples and the Maharawal's palace. Because so many people live here, water use and drainage have become a real threat to its foundations, and conservation groups are working to protect it.

History

Rawal Jaisal, a Bhati Rajput chief, founded the fort and the city named after him in 1156, moving his capital from Lodurva. The site sat on the caravan routes that carried silk, spices and opium between India, Persia and Central Asia, and the trade made Jaisalmer rich.

The fort survived several sieges, including one by the armies of Alauddin Khalji at the end of the 13th century. In the 1570s Jaisalmer made peace with the Mughal emperor Akbar, and in 1818 it signed a treaty with the British. After independence it became part of Rajasthan in 1949. Satyajit Ray's film Sonar Kella (1974) made the fort famous across India.
