About the site

Mandir Palace became the home of the royal family when they left the crowded fort. Its long sandstone front is lined with jharokhas and carved screens, and its courtyards are cooler and more spacious than the old palace above.

Its best-known landmark is the Tazia Tower of Badal Vilas, a slim five-storey tower in which each level has its own balcony and becomes narrower as it rises. Part of the palace is now a heritage hotel, and part is still lived in by the royal family.

History

In the 19th century the Maharawals built a new palace outside the fort walls, with more space and better water supplies. The name Mandir, "temple", comes from the shrines within the complex.

The Tazia Tower is said to have been built by Muslim craftsmen, who gave it to the ruler. Its tiers recall a tazia, the decorated model of a tomb carried during Muharram processions.
