Five mansions of a brocade merchant

Style
Rajasthani haveli

Daily, about 9:00 am – 6:00 pm

About the site

Patwon ki Haveli is a group of five havelis, or mansions, built side by side in a narrow lane. Their sandstone fronts are covered from top to bottom with jharokhas, carved brackets and lattice screens, so that the whole street looks like a wall of lace.

The first and largest haveli is open as a museum. Its rooms show how a rich merchant family lived, with painted ceilings, mirror work, brass utensils and traditional furniture.

History

Guman Chand Patwa made his fortune trading in brocade and gold and silver thread, which is where the name Patwa comes from. In 1805 he began building a haveli for each of his five sons, and the work took around fifty years to complete.

As trade routes changed in the 19th century, the family's fortunes faded and they moved away. Today the havelis are among the most photographed buildings in Rajasthan.
