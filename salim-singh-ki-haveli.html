Salim Singh ki Haveli

The prime minister's peacock mansion

Built
1815
Built by
Salim Singh Mehta, prime minister (diwan) of Jaisalmer
Style
Rajasthani haveli
Location
Near the eastern side of the fort
Features
Moti Mahal top floor, 38 balconies
Visiting hours
Daily, about 8:00 am – 6:00 pm
Note
Still partly lived in by descendants

About the site

Salim Singh ki Haveli is easy to spot because its top storey is wider than the floors below, jutting out over the street on carved brackets. This upper floor, the Moti Mahal, has a row of balconies and a roof shaped in curved arches, said to resemble a peacock.

The haveli has 38 balconies, each with a different carved design. The lower floors are much plainer, which makes the ornate top look even richer.

History

Salim Singh Mehta was the diwan, or prime minister, of Jaisalmer in the early 19th century, and one of the most powerful men in the state. He built this haveli in 1815 over part of an older 17th-century house.

Legend says he wanted it to rise as high as the Maharawal's palace in the fort, and that the ruler had the extra storeys stopped. Salim Singh is also remembered for his heavy taxes, which are often blamed for the abandonment of Kuldhara and other Paliwal villages.
