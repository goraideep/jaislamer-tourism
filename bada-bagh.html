Bada Bagh

Royal cenotaphs on the hill

Built
From the 16th century
Built by
Maharawal Lunakaran, in memory of his father Jait Singh II
Style
Royal cenotaphs (chhatris)
Location
About 6 km north of the city
Getting there
Taxi or auto-rickshaw
Visiting hours
Daily, about 8:00 am – 6:00 pm
Best time
Sunset

About the site

Bada Bagh, the "big garden", is a group of chhatris, domed pavilions built as memorials to the rulers of Jaisalmer and their families. They stand in rows on a low hill above a dam and a garden. Each chhatri has carved pillars and a dome, and many have an inscribed stone slab inside.

The site is famous at sunset, when the golden domes stand out against the sky. Behind them, wind turbines now line the ridge, making it a striking mix of old and new.

History

Maharawal Jait Singh II began building a dam and garden here in the early 16th century. After his death, his son Lunakaran completed the garden and built a chhatri in his memory. Later rulers were also remembered here, so the number of cenotaphs grew over the centuries.

The dam held water for the garden, which once grew fruit and vegetables for the city. Some of the later chhatris were never finished.

Timeline

  1. Early 16th c.Jait Singh II begins the dam and garden
  2. 16th c.Lunakaran completes the garden and his father's chhatri
  3. 16th – 20th c.Cenotaphs added for later rulers
