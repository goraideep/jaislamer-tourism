About the site

Nathmal ki Haveli is a five-storey mansion carved from a single colour of yellow sandstone. Two life-size stone elephants stand at the entrance as guards, and the front is covered with jharokhas, flowers and figures.

Look closely and the left and right halves do not quite match. Inside, wall paintings show horses and cattle alongside newer things the carvers had only heard about, such as bicycles, cars and steam engines.

History

The haveli was built in 1885 for Diwan Mohata Nathmal, the prime minister of Jaisalmer. The work was given to two brothers, Hathi and Lulu, who each took one half of the building.

They worked at the same time but separately, without fully matching their designs. The small differences between the two sides are now the haveli's most famous feature.
