About the site

Kuldhara is a ghost village of roofless stone houses laid out along straight streets. Doorways, stairways, cooking places and grain stores are still easy to see, along with a small temple near the centre. Some houses have been restored so visitors can picture how a family once lived here.

The village is quiet and windswept, and its reputation as a haunted place draws many visitors. The best time to come is late afternoon, when the low sun brings out the colour of the stone.

History

Kuldhara was founded by Paliwal Brahmins, a community of skilled farmers and traders who knew how to grow crops in the desert. For about six hundred years it was a prosperous village, one of a cluster of 84 Paliwal settlements around Jaisalmer.

Around 1825 the people of Kuldhara and the nearby villages left, all at once. Legend says they fled the cruel diwan Salim Singh, who demanded the village chief's daughter, and that they cursed the land so no one could settle there again. Historians point to heavy taxes, falling water supplies and possibly an earthquake as more likely causes.
