Lace carved in yellow stone

Built
12th – 16th century
Built by
Jain merchant families of Jaisalmer
Style
Dilwara-style Jain temple architecture
Location
Inside Jaisalmer Fort
Dedicated to
Tirthankaras including Parshvanath, Rishabhanath and Sambhavnath
Visiting hours
Mornings, about 8:00 am – 12:00 noon
Note

About the site

Inside the fort stands a group of seven Jain temples joined by corridors and stairways. Every surface is carved: pillars, ceilings, brackets and walls are covered with dancers, musicians, animals, flowers and figures of the Tirthankaras, the great teachers of the Jain faith. The carving is so fine that the stone looks almost like lace.

The largest temple is dedicated to Parshvanath, the 23rd Tirthankara. Below the Sambhavnath temple is the Gyan Bhandar, a library that holds palm-leaf and paper manuscripts, some of them many centuries old.

History

Jaisalmer's wealth came from trade, and many of its richest traders were Jain. Over about four hundred years they paid for these temples, which is why styles change from one shrine to the next while still sharing the same golden stone.

Jain monks gathered and protected rare manuscripts here, especially in times of war. The Gyan Bhandar collection is one of the most important Jain libraries in India and is still looked after by the Jain community.
